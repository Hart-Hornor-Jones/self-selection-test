Admit rates by how many students apply

Every California public high school that sent at least 30 applications to a University of California campus in 2023–2025. Schools are sorted into five groups by what share of their UC-eligible seniors applied, and into four groups by how their own students scored on the state tests. Pick a campus, then hover or click any point to see exactly which schools it stands for.

    How the groups are built. A school’s application rate is the number of applications it sent to the campus divided by the number of its seniors who completed the “A–G” course sequence UC requires — roughly, the share of college-eligible students who applied. A school’s test performance is the share of its 11th graders meeting the state standard on the CAASPP English and math tests, averaged. Both cuts are made separately within each campus, so the five columns and four lines always hold about the same number of schools. Cells containing fewer than eight schools are not drawn.

    Sources. University of California Information Center, source-school admissions files; California Department of Education CAASPP results, A–G completion files, and CALPADS unduplicated pupil counts. Figures pool the 2023, 2024 and 2025 admission cycles. Counts of applications and offers are sums; rates are averages across the schools in a group, so a large school and a small one count equally.

    What this page does not do. It shows what the published records contain. It does not model why any school’s rate is what it is, and a difference between two groups here is not by itself evidence about how any individual application was read.
